About Me

I help research teams and clinical labs who have the biology expertise and the data, but need someone to design, build, or modernise their bioinformatics infrastructure. With nearly 20 years at the intersection of biology, computing, and systems architecture, I turn raw sequencing data into analytical workflows that hold up in production.

On one side, code: Bash, AWK, Perl, Python, Groovy, Java, C. On another, infrastructure: HPC, containers, cloud. In the middle, 20 years of genomics. I design Nextflow pipelines on Google Cloud, Azure, and OVHcloud, and help teams figure out what compute setup actually makes sense for their data and their budget.

I can work with nf-core and the Seqera Platform when they fit the context, but I also build lightweight custom Nextflow pipelines from the DSL up, when teams need more control, lower cost, or closer alignment with their biology.

My bioinformatics experience covers HLA typing, variant calling, structural variant analysis, annotation workflows (VEP, SnpEff), quality control (FastQC, MultiQC, Mosdepth, CoverM), expression analysis (Cufflinks, featureCounts), metagenomics, and proteomics. I've used DRAGEN extensively, and work daily with BWA, minimap2, HISAT2, samtools, bedtools, and GATK.

Whether it's tuning a SLURM scheduler, writing a Nextflow pipeline, or crafting an AWK one-liner, I go straight to the root of the problem. KIS — Keep It Simple — is not a platitude, it's how I actually work.

My background in Perl, C, and C++ is a genuine asset: I've been asked more than once to modernise legacy pipelines built in these languages, and knowing them deeply — their idioms, their performance characteristics, their failure modes — means I can migrate them faithfully to Nextflow without losing the intent of the original logic. Most bioinformaticians today have never had to debug a segfault or optimise a tight Perl loop. I have, and that matters when the job is to rewrite something that works and make it work better.
